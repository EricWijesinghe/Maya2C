## In plain words

Maya2C keeps its shared notebook with a **group of validators**. Each one is a
computer that keeps a copy, signs pages, and helps seal them. The more honest
validators run by different people, the harder it is for anyone to cheat.

To become one you need three things: a **key** (a secret you keep on your
computer, like the key to a safe), a computer that stays on, and a seat that
the group accepts. A seat that never answers counts against the group. So new
seats are checked before they are added.

:::caution[No money, no tokens]
Testnet coins have **no value**. Running a testnet validator earns public
recognition only. Nothing on this site promises tokens, money or a mainnet
allocation, and anyone who says otherwise is not speaking for this project.
:::

### Why registration is reviewed for now

Today every validator seat carries one vote, whatever its stake. A registration
whose node never comes online still takes a seat, and with enough absent seats
no quorum can form and the chain stops
([ADR-039](https://github.com/EricWijesinghe/Maya2C/blob/master/docs/adr/ADR-039-quorum-n-minus-f.md)).
The arithmetic is in the scientists tab below.

Until votes are weighted by stake, the testnet validator only proposes
registrations above a bond floor that the faucet cannot fund. The project funds
the bond of each approved operator. This is a stopgap for a testnet. It is not
how mainnet will work.

## Requirements

- An always-on machine. Measured on 2026-10-04 after 3 h 47 min and 12,277
  blocks on the testnet validator: 39 MB of memory, 59 s of CPU time, and 150 MB
  of data, about 1 GB a day at that rate with empty blocks. Busier blocks need
  more. Plan disk for growth or run with pruning.
- A stable connection, and an inbound TCP port you can open.
- Willingness to update within 48 hours of a release marked *required*.

## Steps

1. **Install** `maya2c-node` and `l1-wallet` from the
   [releases page](https://github.com/EricWijesinghe/Maya2C/releases), and check
   them against `SHA256SUMS`.
2. **Make a validator key.** This prints its public key:

   ```bash
   maya2c-node --generate-validator-key validator.key
   ```

   The file is the secret. Keep it on the validator machine only, readable by you
   alone, and back it up offline. Losing it costs your seat. Leaking it lets
   someone sign as you, which is slashed as double signing.
3. **Make a wallet** with `l1-wallet generate` (it writes `wallet.key` by
   default), then note the address with `l1-wallet address`.
4. **Apply** by opening a
   [validator application](https://github.com/EricWijesinghe/Maya2C/issues/new?template=validator-application.yml)
   with the public key and the address. Never paste a key file.
5. **Once approved and funded, register:**

   ```bash
   l1-wallet --rpc-url https://rpc.maya2c.dev/rpc register-validator \
     --validator-key validator.key --bond <the amount agreed>
   ```

   The flags are those in `bins/l1-wallet/src/main.rs`: `--validator-key`,
   `--bond` (required), `--commission-bps` (default 0). The command prints your
   validator id. The committee is recomputed at each epoch boundary, every 3,600
   blocks, and you join it at the first boundary after the registration is final.
6. **Get the testnet genesis** from
   [maya2c.dev/testnet/genesis.json](/testnet/genesis.json) and check that it hashes
   to `dd9bb356c94b7699e6e8d2595681faee48be435b02dc927677cbf833e55edd5d` (SHA-256):

   ```bash
   sha256sum genesis.json
   ```

7. **Run the node.** It dials the bootnode over WebSocket, so it works from behind
   NAT. Flags from `print_usage` in `bins/maya2c-node/src/main.rs`:

   ```bash
   maya2c-node --genesis genesis.json --data-dir data \
     --validator-key validator.key \
     --bootnode /dns4/p2p.maya2c.dev/tcp/443/wss/p2p/12D3KooWJqH5AUQ1zPkMke18rcc8VHN2TWQ5XudGX3tP8zPLuyjB \
     --prune-depth 3600
   ```

   - `--prune-depth 3600` prunes block bodies older than 3,600 blocks; it implies
     `--prune`.
   - To start from a peer's state snapshot rather than replaying every block, add
     `--bootstrap-from <peer JSON-RPC URL>`.
   - Without `--validator-key` the node only observes. Never run one key on two
     machines.

   A fresh node run this way on 2026-10-04 loaded the snapshot at height 52,414,
   caught up 458 attested blocks and was following the tip (57,139 against 57,140).

### Useful flags

| Flag | Effect |
|---|---|
| `--version` | Prints the binary name and version, and exits. Only as the first argument. |
| `--config <PATH>` | Operational settings file; flags override it |
| `--print-config-template` | Prints the default template |
| `--min-register-bond <N>` | Never propose a registration bonding less than N (ADR-039) |
| `--catch-up-from <URL>` | Rejoin through a peer's attested checkpoint (ADR-038); repeatable |
| `--snapshot-interval <N>` | Snapshot every N blocks; validators default to 3,600, 0 turns it off |
| `--remote-signer <ADDR>` | Sign through `maya2c-signer` instead of a key file (ADR-033) |

## What being a validator means

- **Downtime is slashed.** A validator that commits fewer than half of the anchor
  slots it led in an epoch loses 1% of its bond and is jailed for two epochs.
- **Double signing is slashed hard.** 50% of the bond, and the key is retired.
- **Your node votes on every round.** Its signatures are ML-DSA-65, the NIST
  post-quantum standard, like every other signature on the chain.

## The halting condition

Let $m$ be the number of online seats and $k$ the number of absent seats, so the
committee has $N = m + k$ seats. From [ADR-039](https://github.com/EricWijesinghe/Maya2C/blob/master/docs/adr/ADR-039-quorum-n-minus-f.md),
$f = \lfloor (N-1)/3 \rfloor$ and the quorum is $N - f$. The online seats can form a
quorum alone exactly when $m \ge N - f$, that is, when

$$
k \le \left\lfloor \frac{N-1}{3} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{m + k - 1}{3} \right\rfloor .
$$

The chain halts when $k$ exceeds that bound. Two examples from ADR-039:

| Setup | $m$ | $k$ | $N$ | $f$ | Halts? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| One validator plus one absent registration | 1 | 1 | 2 | 0 | yes ($k > 0$) |
| Four validators plus two absent registrations | 4 | 2 | 6 | 1 | yes ($k > 1$) |
| Four validators plus one absent registration | 4 | 1 | 5 | 1 | no ($k \le 1$), but anchor slots are missed |

Below the halting threshold, up to $f$ absent seats still slow the chain: their
anchor slots are missed. That is the case the downtime penalty is built for.

## The penalties

Let $B$ be a bond. The parameters are in `crates/staking/src/params.rs`, in basis
points where $10\,000$ bps is 100%:

| Offence | Test | Penalty | Parameter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Downtime | led $< 5\,000$ bps of its anchor slots in the epoch | $B \cdot 100 / 10\,000 = 0.01\,B$ | `downtime_slash_bps = 100`, `downtime_threshold_bps = 5000` |
| Downtime | | jailed for $2$ epochs | `jail_epochs = 2` |
| Double signing | two signed vertices for one slot | $B \cdot 5\,000 / 10\,000 = 0.5\,B$, key retired | `double_sign_slash_bps = 5000` |

Double signing is the attack on the quorum argument in
[consensus](/guides/consensus/), and its penalty is ten times the downtime one.

## Why the seat is limited

Under equal weights a seat is one vote regardless of bond. An attacker who
registers $k$ idle keys therefore buys $k$ votes for the cost of $k$ bonds.
Stake-weighted quorums ([ADR-040](https://github.com/EricWijesinghe/Maya2C/blob/master/docs/adr/ADR-040-stake-weighted-committee.md))
change the unit of the threshold from seats to weight $W$, with $f = \lfloor (W-1)/3 \rfloor$.
The attacker then needs roughly a third of the **stake**, not a third of the
seats. The reviewed registration on the testnet is a stopgap until that lands.
