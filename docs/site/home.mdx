<h2 class="maya-section-title">Security you can check, not take on trust</h2>

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Two signatures per transaction" icon="seti:lock">
    ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) and SLH-DSA-SHA2-128s (FIPS 205), and both must
    verify. 11,165 bytes per transaction: the price of not betting the chain on
    one lattice assumption.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Finality in seconds" icon="rocket">
    A certified DAG ordered by Bullshark. A block is derived from committed
    certificates, never proposed, and a validator restarted from its safety
    log cannot equivocate. Tested with one of four validators killed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Post-quantum transport" icon="random">
    libp2p with an ML-KEM-768 layer over Noise, and an optional HQC-192 second
    KEM behind a flag.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Honest by construction" icon="information">
    Every subsystem is labelled verified, working, simulated or research in a
    machine-checked ledger. A claim without a test fails CI, and so does the
    word "first" without a dated search of who else did it.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<h2 class="maya-section-title">Detached apps on one identity</h2>

An identity is an ML-DSA-65 key, and its address is the chain's own. The same
key can sign a transaction, open a Maya Chat session or log in to the next
app, and none of those apps links the node. Each can be replaced without
touching the others.

    Research status: unaudited.
