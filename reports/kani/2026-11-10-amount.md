# Kani: maya-amount, 2026-11-10

Every `#[kani::proof]` in `crates/amount/src/proofs.rs`, run one at a time
under a 20-minute cap each, plus the crate added to the nightly Kani matrix.

## Setup

- Base: `origin/master` d975d326, branch `proofs/amount-kani`. `crates/amount`
  is byte-identical between d975d326 and 392fa94e (where batch 1 started,
  from a stale local `master`); checked with `git diff --stat`.
- cargo-kani 0.67.0 (`cargo kani --version`), the same version CI installs.
- WSL2 Ubuntu-24.04, kernel 6.18.33.1-microsoft-standard-WSL2, Intel Core
  Ultra 9 275HX, 24 threads, 11 GiB RAM visible to WSL. The machine was
  shared with other agents' builds through the slot lock.
- Command per harness (`CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/root/kani-target-amount`):
  `cargo kani -p maya-amount --harness proofs::<name> --exact`, killed by
  process group at 1200 s.
- "Wall" includes recompiling the crate (~60 s from `/mnt/d`); "CBMC" is
  Kani's own `Verification Time` line.

## What the brief asked for, and what was already there

The brief asked for a new `cfg(kani)` proofs module. It already existed:
`crates/amount/src/proofs.rs` has had 14 harnesses since 2026-10-18/19
(commits 288210d3, 0b5a63c0, c1b0a7fc), with the `#[cfg(kani)] mod proofs;`
line already in `lib.rs`. What was actually missing:

1. **No CI ran them.** `maya-amount` was not in the nightly Kani matrix.
   Added it. The five no-verdict harnesses go into the crate's heavy list
   (nightly skips them) and into `kani-heavy.yml` (one runner each, a
   300-minute budget), so none is dropped.
2. **checked_add / checked_sub**: already proved at full width, `None`
   exactly on overflow or underflow. Added the saturating forms against the
   checked ones, also at full width.
3. **Ordering**: `cmp` was already proved to be the integer order. Added
   `eq_partial_cmp_and_cmp_agree`: the derived `PartialEq`, the hand-written
   `PartialOrd` and `cmp` agree, and `cmp` reverses when the operands swap.
4. **Decimal parse/format**: the crate has **no parser**, only `Display`, which
   writes base units with no decimal point (the 24 decimals of
   `COIN_DECIMALS` are not applied here). Adding a parser would be non-proof
   code in a crate the frozen node links, so the proofs carry their own
   independent reader. The round trip in practice:
   - `decimal_chunk_step_is_exact_below_2_64` (verified): one
     `div_rem_u64(10¹⁹)` step is exact and leaves a remainder below 10¹⁹,
     for every amount below 2⁶⁴.
   - `decimal_chunk_step_is_exact` (full width) and the end-to-end
     `display_round_trips_through_a_decimal_reader` (below 10⁸): no verdict
     in 20 minutes. Why each bound was chosen is written in the doc comment
     of each harness.
5. **Mul/div at bounded width**: the three bounded mul/div harnesses that
   exist (64 × 64, 128 × 128, 256 × u64) did not finish in 20 minutes. The
   first two were already recorded in `proofs.rs` as not finishing in 20
   minutes on 2026-10-18. Full-width multiplication is proved instead, row by
   row (`mul_row_*`, `mul_step_fits_u128`), and division step by step
   (`long_division_step_keeps_the_invariant`). Each of those finished in
   under 70 s of CBMC time.

## Why the decimal round trip is expensive

The harness has one `#[kani::unwind]` bound for all its loops. `Display`'s
chunk loop and its padded-write loop exit on symbolic conditions, so CBMC
unrolls both, with `core::fmt`'s digit and padding loops nested inside, out
to that one bound. At unwind 25, enough for 19-character zero padding, the
first form never left symbolic execution. At unwind 9 it reaches the solver
with 7.3 M variables. The remaining hard case is the same one that stalls
the multiply harnesses: showing a divider circuit equal to a multiplier.
